# Routing exercise: 2026-10-15

## Method and limits

Five execution routes received the same isolated packet: a specified row transformation, a per-key asynchronous persistence repair, and an upgrade review based on the plugin's installer pattern. Sol Reviewer reviewed the persistence starter and upgrade case without editing. Each route ran once, with no Lead hints or repair. Model/effort came from runtime turn records, not task names or agent claims.

This is a small, tightly specified exercise, not an overall intelligence ranking or a test of autonomous work across a large repository. The persistence requirements explicitly named important failure paths. All routes identifying them does not measure unaided discovery equally well. Host instructions and tools add overhead; runs were not randomized or repeated.

## Routing decision

- Narrow Luna's default scope in response to the user's production experience. Use it for low-risk extraction, formatting, edits following an approved pattern and prescribed checks. Settled procedures may include simple logic; this test does not show that Luna cannot reason.
- Independent diagnosis, design and shared-state changes go to capable Sol or Lead. A short diff or existing tests alone does not make a task suitable for Luna.
- Retain Sol Medium for clear light reasoning and High for normal independent execution. One easy sample does not justify changing the general default. XHigh remains for complex work/review and Max for work expected to exceed XHigh; the deeper follow-up below also tested both execution efforts.
- Astra Low and Medium showed no delivery-quality advantage over Sol on this sample. Keep Low selective. Medium remains a useful controller choice and was tested as a comparator, not added as an automatic extra worker or escalation step.
- Reuse capable context. Determine fit from comparable real outcomes; official benchmarks and Radar are advisory, never execution acceptance criteria. Do not run this exercise on every task.

## Deeper follow-up requested by the user

The user correctly noted that the first packet was too tightly specified to distinguish deeper judgment. A second packet and its private acceptance rubric were frozen before reading its answers. Agents received no hints, scores or corrective feedback. Existing capable agents were reused; Sol XHigh and Max workers were new contexts. This saves repeated setup but means warm-context latency/cache comparisons are not controlled model benchmarks. Luna also had a documentation turn between rounds; its counters below exclude that work.

- D: repair a document autosave coordinator with revision-specific waiters, coalescing, concurrent documents, acknowledgement validation, synchronous/asynchronous failures and explicit retry. Ten independent behavioral groups were applied to each submitted implementation. No Lead repair was made.
- E: reason about an outbox with 24-hour provider deduplication, a 30-hour outage, arbitrarily delayed workers, reusable owners and no shared transaction. The rubric required distinguishing achievable local guarantees from impossible permanent external guarantees, with concrete counterexamples and product decisions.
- F: produce an optimal plan for nine dependent tasks and a shared-file writer exclusion. The correct optimum is 19 units, achieved with three workers; a shared-writer/dependency lower bound also rules out improvement with 25 workers.
